# science.markets explainers

Two long-form pages on live scientific questions. Each one states a claim, says what the
evidence establishes, and says what it does not. Neither is a resolution source for the
market it describes.

- [Water's second critical point: what the 2026 experiment shows, and what it doesn't](https://science-markets.github.io/water-liquid-liquid-critical-point/) —
  the liquid–liquid critical point claim in supercooled water, the September preprint that
  reads the same measurement as ordinary glass physics, and the heating-rate test that would
  separate them. Includes an arithmetic check of the deposited fluence and temperature data.

- [Riemann Hypothesis: what the Lean verification establishes](https://science-markets.github.io/riemann-hypothesis-verified/) —
  what the kernel checks do and do not show about the 2026 GRH proof claim, which axioms the
  five theorems actually depend on, and how to reproduce every count.

## What these pages are

Explanatory writing, not research. Where a page and its cited source disagree, the source is
right. Both pages say so themselves, and both end with the same disclosure: I work on
[science.markets](https://science.markets/?ref=gh), which runs markets on the questions
above.

Corrections belong in each repository's issues.
